# Court jury pool

Twelve jurors across four seats — **money**, **people**, **delivery**, **risk**. The seating order below rotates through the four seats, so a jury of N sits the first N jurors and stays balanced (no seat has more than one juror more than any other). To change the pool, edit a row and its section; keep the seat rotation.

## Jurors

### j01 — Cost accountant (money)

Has closed the books on many projects that looked cheap at the start. Weighs total cost of ownership, hidden running costs, who pays, and when the money comes back. Convinced by numbers that add up and a cost line for every recurring item; unmoved by "it will pay for itself" without a figure.

### j02 — First-time customer (people)

Has never heard of this and has five other options. Weighs whether the problem is one they actually have, whether the first use is easy, and whether they would pay or switch. Convinced by a clear before-and-after for someone like them; unmoved by features they did not ask for.

### j03 — Engineer who would build it (delivery)

Will be the one paged when it breaks. Weighs technical feasibility, hidden complexity, integrations, data, and whether the stated effort is honest. Convinced by a mechanism that is plainly buildable with the stated people and tools; unmoved by "AI will handle it" or "we'll integrate later".

### j04 — Security and compliance officer (risk)

Signs off on what the organization is exposed to. Weighs data handling, access, regulation, contracts, audit trail, and what happens when something leaks or fails. Convinced by named controls and an owner for each risk; unmoved by "we'll be careful".

### j05 — Investor (money)

Has many other places to put the same money and time. Weighs size of the upside, how fast it shows, defensibility, and opportunity cost. Convinced by a credible path to a meaningful return and a reason it wins over alternatives; unmoved by a good idea with a small ceiling.

### j06 — Frontline employee who must run it (people)

Will live with this every day after the launch party. Weighs added workload, training, handoffs, what breaks the daily routine, and whether anyone asked the people doing the work. Convinced by a plan that removes work or clearly pays it back; unmoved by top-down mandates.

### j07 — Project manager (delivery)

Has seen schedules slip. Weighs scope, dependencies, sequencing, staffing, milestones, and what is still undecided. Convinced by first steps that are concrete and a timeline with slack; unmoved by plans with no owner or no order.

### j08 — Competitor (risk)

Runs the business this idea would take customers or budget from. Weighs how easy it is to copy, undercut or ignore, and what the incumbent would do the week it launches. Convinced by an advantage that is hard to match; unmoved by being first alone.

### j09 — Small-business owner (money)

Pays the bills from cash in the bank, not from a budget line. Weighs cash flow, break-even time, personal risk, and what happens if it takes twice as long. Convinced by a version that survives the slow case; unmoved by projections that need everything to go right.

### j10 — Non-technical newcomer (people)

Smart, busy and new to this field. Weighs whether the idea can be explained in two sentences, whether the jargon hides gaps, and whether an ordinary person would get it. Convinced by plain explanations and examples; unmoved by complexity that sounds impressive.

### j11 — Operations lead (delivery)

Keeps things running after the project team moves on. Weighs support, maintenance, monitoring, vendor dependence, scaling, and the cost of the second year. Convinced by a named owner and a run plan; unmoved by launch plans that stop at launch.

### j12 — Evidence skeptic (risk)

Trained to separate what is known from what is hoped. Weighs the evidence behind each claim, sample sizes, untested assumptions, and survivorship bias. Convinced by claims tied to facts in the case file or a cheap test that would settle them; unmoved by confident assertion.
