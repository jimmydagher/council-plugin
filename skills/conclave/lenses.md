# Conclave lens pool

Twelve lenses in six **counterweight pairs**. Each lens is a way of looking at an idea, not a person; each is paired with the lens that pushes hardest against its blind spot. Seat numbers 1–2, 3–4, … 11–12 are the pairs, so a panel of N sits the first N lenses and forms as many whole pairs as possible (an odd N leaves the last lens without its counterweight). To change the pool, edit a row and its section; keep each pair on seat numbers 2k−1 and 2k.

## Lenses

### l01 — Customer advocate

**Method:** Starts from the person the idea is for: what job they are trying to get done, what they do today, and what would make them switch. **Blind spot:** Loves what users ask for and can ignore whether anyone can afford to deliver it. **Must answer:** Who exactly wants this, and what do they do instead today?

### l02 — Unit economist

**Method:** Reduces the idea to money per unit: what one customer, transaction or hour costs and returns, what scales and what does not, and when the cash comes back. **Blind spot:** Can kill an idea whose value is real but not yet measurable in dollars. **Must answer:** What does one unit cost and earn, and what has to be true for that to be positive?

### l03 — Systems thinker

**Method:** Looks at second-order effects: who else is affected, what feedback loops start, what the idea changes around itself, and what the system will do to the idea in response. **Blind spot:** Sees interactions everywhere and can bury a simple move under complexity. **Must answer:** What changes elsewhere because of this, and how does that change come back?

### l04 — Pragmatic builder

**Method:** Looks for the smallest thing that could be built or tried next week, what it would teach, and what can be faked, bought or skipped at first. **Blind spot:** Rewards what is easy to ship and can underweight what only pays off at scale. **Must answer:** What is the smallest real test, and what would it prove?

### l05 — Skeptic

**Method:** Hunts for the assumptions the idea stands on, separates what is known from what is hoped, and asks for the cheapest evidence that would settle each. **Blind spot:** Can stall on proof for choices that are cheap to reverse. **Must answer:** Which assumption, if wrong, breaks the idea, and what is the evidence for it today?

### l06 — First-principles rebuilder

**Method:** Throws away how it is usually done, restates the goal from scratch, and rebuilds the idea from the facts that are actually fixed. **Blind spot:** Reinvents what the field already learned the hard way. **Must answer:** If we started from the goal with no precedent, would we arrive at this idea?

### l07 — Downside guardian

**Method:** Asks what could ruin the idea or the people behind it: rare but severe failures, irreversible steps, single points of failure, and what is hard to undo. **Blind spot:** Sees fragility in everything and can price in disasters that never come. **Must answer:** What is the worst realistic outcome, and can we survive and recover from it?

### l08 — Cost-of-inaction advocate

**Method:** Treats doing nothing as a choice with a price: what is lost each month of delay, what window closes, and what the status quo quietly costs. **Blind spot:** Pushes for speed and can under-count the damage of a bad launch. **Must answer:** What does waiting cost, and by when does that cost become real?

### l09 — Daily operator

**Method:** Imagines year two: who runs this every day, what breaks the routine, what needs training, support and monitoring, and who owns it when the project team leaves. **Blind spot:** Defends the way things run now and can resist change that is worth the disruption. **Must answer:** Who owns this after launch, and what does a bad Tuesday look like?

### l10 — Market strategist

**Method:** Places the idea among alternatives and rivals: how easily it is copied or ignored, what advantage lasts, and where the idea fits in the longer plan. **Blind spot:** Over-reads competitors and can chase positioning over real customer value. **Must answer:** Why does this win, and what stops someone else from doing it better?

### l11 — Trust steward

**Method:** Looks at the people and obligations the idea touches: data, fairness, consent, reputation, regulation, and whether affected people would accept it if they saw how it works. **Blind spot:** Can treat every trade-off as a breach and make caution the default. **Must answer:** Who could be harmed or misled, and would they accept it if they saw everything?

### l12 — Long-horizon futurist

**Method:** Projects the idea three to five years out: which trends make it stronger or obsolete, what technology or behavior will shift, and what option it opens or closes. **Blind spot:** Bets on trend lines that bend, and can undervalue what works now. **Must answer:** Will this still make sense in three years, and what does it make possible later?
